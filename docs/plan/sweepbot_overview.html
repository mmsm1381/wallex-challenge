Wallex Sweep Bot

A bot that keeps buy orders waiting 1% below fair value on Wallex. When a large market sell sweeps the order book, one of those orders fills cheaply, and the bot sells back at fair value a few seconds or minutes later. Fast code places the orders. A language model supervises the plan, reads the news and writes the decision log.

The challenge

Rules

  • 3 days of live algorithmic trading on a Wallex trade-credit account of 5,000,000 toman (about 19 USDT).
  • Spot only. Margin is disabled, so the bot can only buy first and sell later.
  • 345 markets are blocked. 120 spot markets stay open, including BTC, ETH, XRP, SOL, PEPE, DOGE, ADA, SHIB, TRX and USDTTMN.
  • The final value counts in toman. Every trade needs a decision log entry.
  • Deliverables: decision logs, a GitHub repo with documentation, a 5-minute demo.

02

What we tried first, and why we dropped it

Every idea was tested against real Wallex fees before any code went live. Most ideas failed that test.

Dropped

Predict the next candle

A LightGBM model on BTC 1-minute features called the next 5-minute candle right 52.6% of the time. A sweep of 2,016 Supertrend settings on 21 coins had no edge after fees.

Dropped

Wallex lags global prices

Wallex moves only 15–35% of a Binance move in the same minute. We built a live shadow tester (lagcatcher) on 5 global exchanges. After fees, the gaps were too small to trade.

Dropped

Triangle loops

Coin/TMN against coin/USDT × USDT/TMN. The loop never beat the 0.73% taker cost.

Not used

Kronos forecast model

An open candle foundation model, run at 640+ backtest fills. Its forecast did not tell good fills from bad ones. Its only signal was recent volatility, and a simple high-low range did better.

Chosen

Catch the sweeps

Rest bids far below fair value and wait. Large market sells push the price down for seconds, then it comes back. This was positive after fees in a 60-day backtest.

03

The sweep idea

  1. Compute fair value from 5 global exchanges and the Wallex USDT rate.

  2. Rest a bid 1.0% below fair value on the best markets. Move it when fair value moves more than 0.2%.

  3. A sweep fills the bid. Someone sold into an empty book. The price is now below fair value for a short time.

  4. Sell at fair value with a maker order. If the price does not come back in 15 minutes, sell at market.

The bot does not predict direction. It provides liquidity at a price where the fee is covered, and it is paid when an impatient seller wants out at once.

When Binance falls in the same minute, the Wallex drop is a real move, not a sweep. The bot stops bidding then.

Why 1.0% and not 1.3%

1.0% gives about twice the trades for the same net per day. That helps the activity score. The cost is a smaller margin over fees and about three times the drawdown. The team chose 1.0% on 2026-10-23, with smaller bids (475,000 toman) spread over 10 markets.

05

How the bot works

The executor never waits for the model. A sweep lasts under 3 seconds and a model call takes seconds, so fast code acts on the last valid plan, and the model only changes the plan.

flowchart LR
  WX[Wallex websocket: books and trades] --> FV[Fair value and filters]
  GL[Binance, Bybit, OKX, KuCoin, Gate] --> FV
  FV --> EX[Executor: bids, exits, risk guard]
  EX -- orders --> BR[Wallex REST broker]
  BR -- fills --> EX
  EX --> DB[(SQLite: orders, trades, logs)]
  DB --> SUP[AI supervisor]
  NEWS[News search] --> SUP
  SUP -- JSON plan --> VAL[Plan validator]
  VAL -- checked plan --> EX
  FV --> TW[No-AI twin, paper]
  TW --> DB
  DB --> PANEL[Web panel and decision log]
fair value = median(global mids) × USDTTMN mid (toman markets only) × exp(premium)

The premium is the rolling 60-minute median gap between Wallex and the raw global price, taken only from books with a spread of 0.3% or less. When USDTTMN is closed at night, the bot uses an implied USDT rate from coin TMN ÷ coin USDT prices.

Bid

Fair value × (1 − 1.0%), rounded down to the price step. 475,000 toman per bid. Requote only if the target moves more than 0.2%, which keeps within Wallex's limit of 20 orders per 10 s.

Exit

A maker sell at the fair value of the fill moment, moved as fair value moves. After 15 minutes, a taker sell. Cooldown of 60 s after a trade, 5 minutes after a loss.

Same code everywhere

Live, paper and replay modes share one executor. Replay runs on recorded order-book files, so any live minute can be rerun exactly. 276 automated tests.

06

The AI part

A frontier language model works as a supervisor, not as a price predictor. It runs through an OpenAI-compatible router: GPT first, Gemini models as fallbacks. A Claude adapter is also built.

What the model does

  • Plan, every 15 minutes and after each alert. It reads equity, open orders, fills, filters, spreads and the USDT state. It can pause all bids, stop toman bids on FX risk, or change the depth per market.
  • News watch, every hour. Web search for shocks to the toman or crypto.
  • Daily review of the trades and decisions.
  • Decision log in plain language for every plan and trade.

What keeps it safe

  • The plan is strict JSON. A validator limits each value to the hard limits (depth 1.0–2.5%, at most 1M toman per bid, at most 10 bids).
  • If the model fails or is slow, the bot keeps the last plan, or the default plan.
  • A spending limit stops model calls at the budget.
  • The twin: a paper copy of the bot with no AI runs on the same live data. The gap between bot and twin measures what the AI changed.

Risk management

GuardRule
Global drop filterNo bids when Binance falls 0.3% or more in 60 s. Wait 120 s.
FX shock filterNo toman bids when USDTTMN moves 0.5% or more in 5 minutes. Wait 10 minutes.
Spread filterNo bid on a market whose spread is too wide for a fair price.
SizeAt most 1,000,000 toman per bid, 10 bids, 40% of equity in USDT.
Time stopEvery position is closed within 15 minutes.
Loss stopsStop for the day at −1.5%. Stop at −2.5% from peak or −4% from start.
End of challenge18:20 UTC: stop bids, sell all coins. 19:30 UTC: sell all USDT, before the 20:00 USDTTMN close. Final value in toman.

A trade the bot did not take, 08:47 UTC

ADATMN dipped to 59,272. The twin's bid was at 59,317 and filled. The bot's bid was at 59,237 and did not.

Both use the same rule: move the bid only when the target moves more than 0.2%. The twin had last moved its bid at 08:35, so the gap was 0.24% and it moved. The bot had moved at 08:39, so the gap was 0.14% and it stayed.

The price did not come back. The twin sold at the 15-minute limit for −0.48%, which is about the fees.

Quiet markets

The backtest expects about 10 fills a day at 1.0%. The live day has been calm. Sweeps come in clusters with long quiet times between them, and one quiet day is inside the normal range of the backtest.

The global drop filter tripped 77 times. Each time, the bot pulled its bids until the move was over.

10

Limits of the evidence

  • Candles do not show the order of trades inside a minute, or the queue of other bids at the same price. Other bots may fill first.
  • The FX filter was chosen after the USDTTMN crash of 2026-10-01 (−7% in one minute). It is partly fitted to that one event.
  • The order-book replay has only 3 good sweeps so far. Each new day of recording makes the check stronger.
  • The market ranking by fill count is in-sample.
  • So far the AI has not changed a plan. The twin comparison needs more trades before it can show an AI effect.
