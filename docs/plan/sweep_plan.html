Wallex Sweep Catcher

We keep buy orders resting far below fair value on Wallex. When a large market sell sweeps the order book, our bid fills, and we sell back at fair value seconds or minutes later. Fast code runs the orders. Claude sets the plan, watches the news, decides on trades that do not recover, and writes the decision log.

The idea

What a sweep looks like

On 2026-10-22 at 07:03:53 UTC, a seller sold BTC with one large market order on BTCTMN. The order walked down the book through 85 trades in 20 milliseconds, down to 21.66B toman, 1.5% below fair value. One trade alone was 645M toman.

2.6 seconds later, buyers paid 22.018B again. A bid resting 1.3% below fair value filled in that sweep, and its sell at fair value filled one second later: +0.82% after fees.

Our bid fills only when a trade prints below our price. Our higher bid has priority, so such a trade means our order filled first and in full.

Risks and limits

What can make the backtest wrong

Partly fitted filter

We chose the USDTTMN filter after we saw the 10-01 crash. The Binance filter has a clear reason, but both use information from inside the same minute.

Minute candles

Candles do not show the order of trades inside a minute. The live order-book test is the final check, and it has only 3 events so far.

Funds are locked

Each open bid locks its toman or USDT. With 5M we can keep about 5 bids. USDT markets also need a USDT balance, which carries FX risk.

Other bots

Other bots can rest bids at the same price before us. Then they fill first. Deeper bids avoid most of this, but fill less often.

Order limits and API access

Wallex allows about 20 order requests per 10 s. Bids must move with fair value. We must also confirm that the order API works from our server.

Toman shocks and news

A news shock can move all toman markets by several percent in one minute. The filter and Claude's news watch reduce this risk, but cannot remove it.

Architecture

Fast code trades, Claude plans

A sweep lasts 0.01 to 3 seconds. An LLM call takes 5 to 30 seconds. So Claude never reacts to a sweep. The executor holds the bids and runs the filters in milliseconds. Claude changes the plan every 15 minutes, or at once after an alert.

Solid arrows: data and orders. Dashed arrows: plans and reviews from Claude, checked by the plan validator.
Trade flow

The life of one trade

  1. PlanClaude picks up to 5 markets, a depth between 1.0% and 2.0% for each, and the size. The validator clamps every value to the hard limits.
  2. Rest the bidThe executor keeps a bid at fair value minus the depth. It moves the bid when fair value moves more than 0.2%, inside the order budget.
  3. FilterThe executor cancels the bid when Binance falls 0.3% or more in a minute, or (toman markets) when USDTTMN moves 0.5% or more in 5 minutes.
  4. FillA sweep fills the bid. The executor places a sell at fair value at once and logs the fill.
  5. Exit at fair valueMost trades end here, seconds to minutes after the fill, with the maker fee.
  6. Hold or cutNo exit after 5 minutes: Claude reads the context (global prices, USDTTMN, news) and answers hold or cut now.
  7. Time limitAfter 15 minutes, the executor sells at the bid as a taker.
  8. LogClaude writes the decision log entry for the trade: why the bid was there, the filters, the decision, the result.
AI part

Claude's four jobs

We use Claude (frontier model, no training of our own). Every Claude output is structured JSON with a fixed schema. Code checks it against the hard limits before it can change anything.

1. Supervisor

Every 15 minutes and on alerts. Input: market state, recent sweeps, fills, and news from web search (Iran FX, sanctions, Wallex announcements). Output: markets, depths, sizes, pause.

2. Hold or cut

For a position that has not reached fair value after 5 minutes. Today, time exits lose −0.2% on average. This is where Claude's judgment can add money.

3. Decision log

One readable entry per trade and per plan change, written from the stored data. The challenge requires this log.

4. Daily review

Compares the live bot with the no-AI twin (fixed 1.3%, same markets) and proposes bounded changes. This measures what the AI added, for the demo.
